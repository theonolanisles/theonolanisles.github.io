Design an opening hatch

Choose four-bar or six-bar. Both use the same door dimensions, pivot-target workflow, coordinate system and animation controls.

1 · Door dimensions

Set the closed hatch in side elevation. The red door origin O = (0,0) is its lower-left corner and remains attached to the hatch throughout its motion.

Closed door: X = 0 to +width, Y = 0 to +thickness. X points right and Y points up. Only the door and the linkage are drawn; no decorative frame blocks.

2 · Preferred pivot locations

A and D are fixed ground pivots. A-B-E is the input link, B-C-P is the coupler, D-C the rocker, E-Q the connecting link, and P-Q is rigidly attached to the door.

Drag any gold target in the drawing or enter X/Y coordinates below. Each default radius is 50 mm. Pivot targets start in the +X, −Y quadrant; the search regions may extend across an axis.

B, C and E are additional six-bar pivots, not separate door pivots. Editing targets invalidates the previous solution.

3 · Opening movement

Enter the rotation magnitude and direction, and the X/Y displacement of the door origin O from closed to open.

The worked example uses approximately 73.49° counterclockwise rotation and moves O approximately 16.31 mm right and 11.78 mm up. The dashed outline shows the specified open pose.

4 · Synthesize pivot locations and lengths

The search begins at preferred targets, then samples progressively farther within all seven pivot regions. Candidates are checked for consistent link lengths over a sampled stroke and agreement with the required open door pose.

Which six-bar topology?

This is a seven-revolute-joint, two-loop six-bar: ground A-D; input ternary A-B-E; coupler ternary B-C-P; rocker D-C; connecting link E-Q; rigid output door P-Q. Other six-bar arrangements can have different capabilities.
