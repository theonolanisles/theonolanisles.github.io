Planar linkage synthesis

Search for 4-bar and 6-bar mechanisms that move a rigid body between two prescribed poses. Specify a preferred location and allowable search radius for each pivot. The search starts at your target locations and explores outwards. View the resulting mechanism and scrub through its motion.

1 · Mechanism and desired motion

World pose: the closed body uses your coordinate system directly. In the open pose each body point is rotated about the origin, then translated by (X,Y). All dimensions are millimetres.

Moving body envelope

This rectangle represents the moving object in its closed position. Its transformation is checked against optional keep-out polygons.

2 · Preferred pivot locations

Coordinates are in the closed position. A and D are fixed ground pivots. P and Q attach to the moving body. For six-bar designs, B, C and E are internal linkage pivots. A target with radius 0 is fixed at that point.

3 · Search and constraints

Intermediate motion targets

Optional constraints on the body reference point at three evenly selected progress positions. These are evaluated along the simulated input crank stroke, not at fixed output angles.

Keep-out geometry (advanced)

Enter an array of polygons. Each polygon is a list of [X,Y] vertices, for example [[[10,10],[60,10],[60,40],[10,40]]]. The body envelope and linkage centrelines must not intersect these polygons at sampled positions.

Set your pivot preferences, then run a search.

Engineering limitation. Candidate geometries are kinematic suggestions, not certified designs. Sampled positions can miss intermediate collisions or singularities. Validate full-stroke motion, link thickness, joint clearances, loads and manufacturability in CAD before construction. No result does not prove the topology is impossible.
